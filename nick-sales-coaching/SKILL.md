# Nick Broekema — Sales Coaching Skill

## Core rules (always apply)

- **Never invent client stories or fabricated case studies.** If a real example would help, use only the verified client results documented below. Flag when a story is needed and ask Nick.
- **Mine the transcripts first.** Nick's actual language, objections he's heard, and advice he's given are stronger than anything invented.
- **ICP-specific language over generic sales advice.** The goal is for the right person to think "he's describing my exact situation."
- **Sales and content are the same system.** Nick never separates them. Advice on sales always connects back to what the content is (or isn't) doing.

---

## Nick's Sales Diagnostic Framework

Nick always maps the full funnel before prescribing anything. The funnel has five checkpoints:

1. **Content → leads** — Is the content attracting the right people or just getting engagement from peers?
2. **Leads → DMs** — Are the right people initiating conversation, or is the DM inbox random?
3. **DMs → sales calls** — Are warm conversations converting to booked calls?
4. **Sales calls → close** — What's the conversion rate? Where does it break?
5. **Close → LTV** — What's the average deal size, duration, and renewal rate?

Nick's diagnostic question: *"Where in the funnel does it actually break down?"*

Most clients think it's a content problem. It's usually a conversion problem — either in the DMs or on the sales call itself.

## The Four Pillars (always present, emphasis shifts per client)

Nick's work always covers:
1. **ICP & LinkedIn Setup** — profile works before any message is sent
2. **Positioning & Clarity** — offer is obvious to the right person
3. **Content** — posts + carousels that attract buyers, not just followers
4. **Sales** — DMs, calls, follow-up, offer packaging

> "Deze drie dingen zijn altijd nodig bij welke klant ik ook heb. Alleen de ene klant heeft meer aandacht nodig bij sales, dan weer meer met content." — Nick, sales call with Koen van der Meer

When coaching a client, identify which pillar is the bottleneck. Prioritize accordingly. Don't rebuild what's working.

## Nick's Offer Structuring Principles

### Duration
- **Minimum: 3 months** — needed for momentum; results don't come in month one
- **Default: 6 months** — better for MRR, less pressure cooker, more runway for goals
- **Ideal for high-ticket B2B: 12 months** — biggest results, highest LTV, renewal more likely

> "Ik begon met 3 maanden en dat was best een pressure cooker. Dan zag ik ook van... mensen weer terugvallen in hun oude gewoontes." — Nick, sales call with Koen van der Meer

### Pricing structure
- Monthly: €2,750–€3,000/month (coaching)
- Upfront payment: discount applied (Nick doesn't memorize exact %, calculates per deal)
- Done-for-you: priced higher, less scalable
- Entry offer (workshop/audit): €150–€500 to warm up cold audiences
- Cohort: €800–€2,000 per seat; 20–80 seats; done bi-monthly

### Offer packaging advice Nick gives clients
- Simplify — most clients offer too much; buyers get confused and say no
- One signature offer > multiple options ("minder is meer")
- Add a low-ticket entry point to build trust before the big ask
- Quarterly or upfront billing preferred over monthly for cash flow
- Make the next step obvious from within the current engagement

## Nick's Sales Call Structure (how he runs his own calls)

### Opening
- Build real rapport — not performative. Nick talks about Spain, padel, their kids, their day.
- Establish shared context: "I went through your profile / content / sales deck before this call."

### Diagnosis
Ask targeted questions, not a script:
- "How many leads are you getting per week?"
- "Where do most of them drop off?"
- "Do you have a DM strategy, or do you wait for inbound?"
- "What does your sales cycle look like from first contact to signed?"
- "What would an ideal 6-month outcome look like for you?"
- "How much time do you have per week for LinkedIn?"
- "What are your best clients worth in revenue?"

### Framing the offer
- Never pitch features — pitch outcome: "Als je vier klanten erbij doet van 35k, wat zou dat betekenen?"
- Do the math in the call: anchor the investment to the upside
- Reference a relevant case study (see below for verified results)
- Show the Notion board + Figma workspace — make it tangible
- Frame it as done-with-you, not done-for-you: "De implementatie, dat doe jij zelf"

### Handling resistance
Common objections Nick encounters and how he handles them:

**"It feels like a step backwards — we'd redo ICP and offer again."**
> "Ik zeg niet dat we alles omgooien. Vaak is het meer van hoe ga je het verpakken en vermarkten."

**"I'm not sure it fits my specific situation."**
Nick identifies the exact bottleneck live: "Ik denk dat jij het especially bij het sales gedeelte meer aandacht nodig hebt." Then narrows the focus to that pillar.

**"I've already invested a lot in coaches/training without results."**
Acknowledge the frustration honestly. Don't promise what you can't deliver. Nick only takes clients he's confident will win: "Als ik met jou geen goede case study heb, dan verkoopt het zichzelf niet verder. Dat vind ik belangrijker dan een snelle kerstverdiener."

**"Will I actually learn enough to close my own deals?"**
Nick's answer: the coaching is the skill transfer — DM scripts, sales call prep, offer language. Not a ghost closer.

**"I want to commit for less time."**
Minimum 3 months. Offer to split into two payments. Remind them that 3 months is when momentum starts, not when results finish.

### Asking for a reaction in the call
Nick always asks before ending: *"Wat is je eerste indruk? Waar gaat je hoofd naartoe?"*

He doesn't push for yes/no. He wants to hear the real objection so he can address it before they hang up.

### Closing
- No hard close; no follow-up pressure games
- Send the deck + short email summary within 24 hours
- Create light urgency with truth: "Ik heb drie plekken vrij voor maart. Twee dagen is genoeg om te beslissen."
- If they're not the right fit, refer someone else (e.g., Marlou Nelissen for pure sales training)

## Nick's DM Strategy (for himself and clients)

### Warm outreach — the only outreach Nick does
- Target: people who commented on posts, engaged with content, or are in clear ICP
- Not cold — never spray-and-pray
- Goal: start a conversation, not pitch immediately

### DM conversation structure (two types)
1. **Closing in the DMs** — for lower-ticket offers or pre-qualified leads who don't need a call
2. **Getting people onto a sales call** — for higher-ticket 1:1 work

Nick has DM scripts he shares with clients via Notion. Always reference these when coaching on DMs.

### Key principle
> "Ik zag ook van... veel conversie, of mogelijke conversie. En ook in het opvolgen van dingen." — Nick on where clients leave money on the table

The follow-up is where most conversions are lost. Nick coaches clients to follow up within 48 hours, briefly, with a specific next step.

## Campaign-Based Selling (Nick's approach for email + LinkedIn)

Nick doesn't spray content and hope. He runs campaigns:
- **Structure**: 3–7 days of aligned content + email sequence targeting a specific offer
- **Email**: short, personal, one idea + one CTA per email; reads like a LinkedIn post
- **Content**: shifts from educational/tofu to proof/case study/social proof during campaign window
- **Urgency**: real scarcity (limited spots, deadline) — never fake

> "Ik was ook altijd van de organische content niet geen raadsing. Dan zie ik gewoon van, ja, met e-mail kun je eigenlijk zoveel doen." — Nick, sales call with Koen van der Meer

## Common Client Mistakes Nick Identifies

These are real patterns from his sessions — not invented:

1. **Avoiding their offer in content** — content is educational but never mentions what they sell
2. **No DM strategy** — waiting for inbound only; leaving warm leads on the table
3. **Offering too much** — buyers get confused, perceive it as too expensive or too complex
4. **Sales calls that pitch instead of diagnose** — talking about features before understanding the pain
5. **No follow-up system** — deals die in the silence after the call
6. **3-month pressure cooker** — too short for real results; clients fall back into old habits
7. **Treating LinkedIn as a fame game** — chasing followers/engagement instead of buyer conversations
8. **Content without a conversion mechanism** — no DM CTA, no clear next step, no offer visible
9. **Discounting too fast** — erodes perceived value before the buyer sees the ROI
10. **Sales decks that are too complex** — people leave thinking "this is more than I need"

## ROI Math (do this in calls and coaching sessions)

Nick always anchors investment to outcome. Example framework:

> Current: 4 clients × €35k = €140k/year
> Goal: +4 clients in 6 months = +€140k
> Investment: €3k/month × 6 = €18k
> Net new: ~€122k
> → "Dat is laaghangende vrucht als we simpele tweaks doen."

Apply this math to each client's numbers. Make it concrete. The investment has to feel small vs. the upside.
