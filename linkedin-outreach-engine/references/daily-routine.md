# Daily Routine & List Building

## The daily routine (default: 5-3-1)

A repeatable daily block, roughly 45-60 minutes:

- **5 comments** on posts from ICPs and peers with reach (see engagement.md for what makes a comment work)
- **3 new conversations** started (warm sources first, cold to fill the gap)
- **1 touchpoint on your pipeline**: process every reply, run the follow-ups that are due

Plus posting per the user's content cadence (typically 3-5x/week, handled outside this skill).

Adapt the numbers to capacity, but never to zero. Someone with 20 minutes does 3-2-1. Someone in sales-sprint mode does 10 comments and 10+ DMs. The routine matters more than the numbers; consistency beats intensity.

**Order matters:** engagement first (warms up your visibility), then outreach, then pipeline. Follow-ups are part of the routine, not an afterthought; most deals close in the follow-up.

## The golden scan (check daily, in this order)

Open LinkedIn and scan five places. No tools required; Sales Navigator is optional, not needed for this.

1. **Post likes and comments** — anyone engaging with your content
2. **Profile views** — they came to look at you, that's intent
3. **Received connection requests** — inbound interest, qualify it
4. **New followers** — they chose to see more of you, that's a signal
5. **Commenters on peer/competitor content** — they're actively discussing your topic

Also worth a weekly pass: your existing network (past clients, colleagues, warm contacts you never followed up with).

For each hit: apply the ICP check, then send a connection request (see below). These people have indirectly seen your profile or content. That's indirect interest; treat it as warm, not cold.

## Sending the connection request

- **Warm sources (from the scan):** add a short note referencing their action. "Thanks for your comment on my post about [topic]" / "I noticed [common interest]. Happy to connect." One or two lines, no pitch in the request.
- **New followers:** connection request with "Thanks for the follow" plus one line of common ground.
- **Cold:** A/B test blank requests vs. a short value line for 2 weeks, keep the winner. Blank often wins on acceptance; the conversation starts after they accept.
- Never pitch inside the connection request. The request opens the door, the first message starts the conversation.

## The ICP check

- Do they have the right role?
- Are they in the right industry?
- Does their company have the right size?
- Check any ICP documentation you have

Not ICP but relevant peer? Still worth engaging (visibility), not worth a sales conversation.

## Cold list building

When warm sources don't fill the daily quota, build a list of key decision makers:

- **LinkedIn search bar** (free): search by role plus industry keywords, filter by location and connections. Enough for a starter list of 20-50 names
- **Sales Navigator** (optional, paid): filter by role, company size, industry, and activity ("posted in last 30 days"). Useful at higher volumes, not required to start
- **Engagement mining**: pull commenters from posts by big accounts in your niche
- **Signal-based**: hiring pushes, funding news, product launches, earnings calls. A signal gives your first message a reason to exist

## The tracker

Minimum viable tracker: a spreadsheet with name, source (like/view/request/cold), date of last touch, next follow-up date, status (new / in conversation / call booked / dead). Update daily as part of the routine. A CRM like Folk or Sales Navigator lists work too. The tool doesn't matter, the habit does.

## Weekly review (10 min, Fridays)

- How many conversations started this week?
- How many replies? Replies ÷ sends = your message quality signal
- How many calls booked?
- Which source produced the best conversations? Do more of that next week.
