# Cold Outreach

Nick's position: inbound first. Cold outreach is for when you're starting out, need clients now, or your warm sources don't fill the daily quota. It works if you're willing to repeat it. Once inbound kicks in, you'll barely need it.

Ground rules:
- Do the research before you write. One specific detail beats a generic opener every time
- Don't expect a high hit rate. That's normal. Volume matters (10+ per day when in sprint mode)
- The goal is a conversation, not a sale. Short and low-pressure
- Track every send and follow up on schedule

**Two delivery routes:**
1. **Connection request first** (free): request → they accept → send the cold DM. Slower, but the accept itself is a micro-yes
2. **InMail** (needs Premium/Sales Navigator): lands directly in their inbox without a connection. Use for high-value targets you can't reach otherwise; the earned-pitch play below fits InMail well

## The standard 4-step cold DM

**Step 1 — Context / research:**
> Hey [First Name], I've been looking into [their company / their content / their recent initiative]. I noticed [specific detail: a post, a launch, a hiring push].

**Step 2 — Show understanding of pain or intent:**
> That tells me [pain or opportunity you inferred].

**Step 3 — Social proof:**
> I recently helped [similar company / ICP] with [similar challenge]. They went from [pain] to [result].

No case studies yet? Frame past work experience as a client success story: "I helped a client remove [bottleneck], leading to [result]."

**Step 4 — Light invitation:**
> Would it be worth a short chat to see if this could work for you too?

## The earned pitch (for senior targets: C-suite, VPs, founders)

Senior decision-makers don't mind being pitched. They mind lazy pitches. They don't have time for two months of coffee-chat foreplay that turns out to be a sale anyway; a direct, well-researched pitch respects their time. But directness only works when you've earned it first.

**The checklist. Before sending a direct pitch, you can honestly say:**

- I researched them AND their business (recent posts, company news, earnings calls, public signals)
- I found a specific pain, backed by something concrete I can point to
- I have something in common with them, or a mutual connection
- I've shown up in their world: engaged with their content, appeared in their feed
- I can give them one insight they didn't have (a number, an observation, a gap you spotted)
- I have a solution that maps to the pain
- My CTA is concrete: two specific time slots, not "let me know if you're open to connect"

**Message structure:**

1. Open with the connection point and get straight to the point (acknowledge they're busy)
2. Name the signal: what you saw or heard that triggered this message
3. Drop the insight: the specific observation with a number or concrete gap
4. Optional: the common ground or mutual contact
5. Concrete CTA: two time slots, offer to send the invite

**Format tip:** a casual video message (recorded walking outside, not a studio production) outperforms text for this play. It proves a human did the homework.

**Effort filter:** this play takes 20-30 minutes of research per prospect. Reserve it for high-value targets where one deal justifies the time. For volume outreach, use the standard 4-step via connection requests.

## Connection request A/B test

Test blank connection requests against a short value line (e.g. "Question about a potential partnership"). Track acceptance rates per variant for 2 weeks, keep the winner.

## Follow-up

Same cadence as warm outreach: day 2, 4, 6, then dead lead. For a "not interested" reply:

> Hey, totally understand. Just to know: what would make this a no-brainer for you?

Their answer is offer research or a reopened conversation. Both beat a polite retreat.
