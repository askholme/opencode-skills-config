# Float slides — storytelling formats

Six narrative structures for slide decks, each with a beat sequence and
suggested float-slides components. Pick the format that matches the audience
and goal; compose slides from `components.md` in beat order.

All formats share the same opening discipline: one idea per slide, eyebrow +
heading + one `em.accent`, copy in the client's language.

---

## 1. TED six-beat

**When to use:** keynotes, conference talks, internal all-hands, any
presentation where the speaker is the primary medium and the slides are
support. Works for 10–45 minutes.

**Structure:**

| Beat | Purpose | Suggested components |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Open with a provocation, a number, or a scene that makes the audience lean in. No agenda slide. | Cover (1), Quote slide (2) |
| **Empathy** | Show you understand the world the audience lives in — their constraints, frustrations, or context. | Eyebrow + Headline (3), Two-column (4), Capability list (6) |
| **Insight** | The single reframe. One sentence that changes how they see the problem. | Dark slide (19), Dark callout (7) |
| **Evidence** | Three to five concrete proofs: data, case studies, demos, comparisons. | Stat grid (15), Timeline (14), Feature card row (23), Code slide (18), Split slide (26) |
| **Vision** | Paint the world after the insight is applied. Make it specific and tangible. | JEDUF three-column (13), Art overlay (25), Hero image (27) |
| **Close** | One ask or one takeaway. Repeat the hook phrase. | Closing (20), Quote slide (2) |

**Notes:** The Hook and Close often use the same image or phrase — the
callback is the signature move of this format. Keep Evidence tight: three
strong proofs beat seven weak ones.

## 2. Sequoia / VC pitch

**When to use:** investor presentations, fundraising decks, partnership pitches
where the audience evaluates risk and return. Typically 10–15 slides, 10–20
minutes.

**Structure:**

| Beat | Purpose | Suggested components |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | The specific pain, with a number attached. Not "the market is large" — "X% of Y do Z manually." | Eyebrow + Headline (3), Stat grid (15), Two-column (4) |
| **Solution** | One sentence. What you do and for whom. | Dark callout (7), Quote slide (2) |
| **Market** | TAM / SAM / SOM or a credible market-sizing argument. | Stat grid (15), Three-column (5) |
| **Product** | How it works. Screenshots, flow, or a live demo. | Product slide (11), Code slide (18), Feature card row (23), Dot flow (8) |
| **Traction** | Evidence the market wants this: revenue, users, retention, logos. | Stat grid (15), Logo bar (22), Testimonial grid (21), Update row (24) |
| **Team** | Why this team, why now. | Logo grid (17), Two-column (4) |
| **Ask** | How much, for what milestones, by when. | Spec block (10), Closing (20) |

**Notes:** Investors read decks before the meeting. Every slide must stand
alone. Put the traction slide early if the numbers are strong — it earns
attention for the rest.

## 3. MBB / McKinsey SCR

**When to use:** strategy recommendations, board presentations, executive
briefings where the audience is senior and time-constrained. The conclusion
comes first; the evidence follows.

**Structure:**

| Beat | Purpose | Suggested components |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | Shared context — what is true and agreed. One slide. | Eyebrow + Headline (3), Two-column (4) |
| **Complication** | What has changed or what is at risk. The reason this meeting exists. | Dark callout (7), Stat grid (15), Timeline (14) |
| **Resolution** | The recommendation. State it plainly in the heading. | Dark slide (19), Spec block (10) |
| **Evidence** | Supporting analysis: data, benchmarks, case studies, risk assessment. | Stat grid (15), Three-column (5), Feature card row (23), Quote pair (16), Timeline (14) |
| **Next steps** | Decisions required, owners, dates. | Update row (24), Dot flow (8), Closing (20) |

**Notes:** The SCR pyramid means the executive can stop reading after the
Resolution slide and still know the answer. Evidence slides are for the
sceptics in the room. Keep the Situation to one slide — if it takes more,
the complication is not sharp enough.

## 4. Product launch

**When to use:** new product or feature announcements, internal launches,
press briefings, customer webinars. Goal: create desire and drive a specific
action.

**Structure:**

| Beat | Purpose | Suggested components |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage** | Set the context: the world before this product existed. | Cover (1), Eyebrow + Headline (3), Quote slide (2) |
| **Reveal** | Name the product. One sentence on what it does. | Dark slide (19), Dark callout (7) |
| **Demo** | Show it working. Screenshots, flow diagrams, or a live walkthrough. | Product slide (11), Feature card row (23), Code slide (18), Split slide (26), Hero image (27) |
| **Impact** | Concrete outcomes: time saved, cost reduced, capability unlocked. Use real numbers. | Stat grid (15), Testimonial grid (21), Quote pair (16) |
| **CTA** | One action: sign up, request access, book a call. | Closing (20), Spec block (10) |

**Notes:** The Reveal slide should feel like a moment — use a Tier 4 Hero
component (Dark slide or Cover) so it reads as a visual break. Keep the Demo
to three to five screens; more than that loses the room.

## 5. Board update

**When to use:** quarterly or monthly board meetings, investor updates,
governance reviews. Audience has context; they want signal, not narrative.

**Structure:**

| Beat | Purpose | Suggested components |
|---|---|---|
| **Summary** | One-slide executive summary: period, headline metric, one risk, one ask. | Eyebrow + Headline (3), Dark callout (7) |
| **KPIs** | Three to five metrics with trend and target. | Stat grid (15), Timeline (14) |
| **Progress** | What shipped or closed since last meeting. | Update row (24), Dot flow (8), Feature card row (23) |
| **Risks** | Two to three risks with owner and mitigation. Be direct. | Two-column (4), Capability list (6), Quote pair (16) |
| **Decisions** | What the board needs to decide or approve today. | Spec block (10), Closing (20) |

**Notes:** Board members read the deck before the meeting. The Summary slide
is the most important — if it is vague, the meeting will be spent
reconstructing context. Risks should name an owner; unowned risks are not
risks, they are complaints.

## 6. Sales deck

**When to use:** outbound pitches, discovery follow-ups, proposal presentations,
renewal conversations. Goal: move the prospect to a decision.

**Structure:**

| Beat | Purpose | Suggested components |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | Mirror the prospect's specific situation back to them. Use their language. | Eyebrow + Headline (3), Two-column (4), Stat grid (15) |
| **Cost of inaction** | What staying the same costs: time, money, risk, opportunity. | Dark callout (7), Quote pair (16), Timeline (14) |
| **Solution** | What you do, for this prospect, in one sentence. | Dark slide (19), Spec block (10) |
| **Proof** | Case studies, logos, testimonials, metrics from similar customers. | Testimonial grid (21), Logo bar (22), Stat grid (15), Quote pair (16) |
| **Proposal** | Scope, price, timeline. One option or a clear anchor + upgrade. | Spec block (10), Three-column (5), Update row (24) |
| **Close** | One next step with a date. Remove friction. | Closing (20), Dark callout (7) |

**Notes:** The Pain slide should feel like the prospect wrote it. If they
nod at slide one, the rest of the deck is confirmation. Keep Proof to two or
three examples that are as close as possible to the prospect's industry and
company size — generic logos are less convincing than one specific story.
