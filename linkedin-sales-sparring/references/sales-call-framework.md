# Sales Call Framework (Nick Broekema)

Based on multiple real sales calls. The structure runs from first impression to signature. The order matters. Adapt language to your own ICP and voice, but keep the order.

## 0. Preparation (before the call — never skip)

- Check their LinkedIn profile and recent posts: what are they posting, what does their offer look like, how much engagement?
- If they sent a deck or website: read it. Reference it in the opening
- Note 1-2 specific things that stood out. This shows you actually looked
- Check if they know anyone in your network

Without preparation you fall back on generic questions. You feel it. They feel it too.

## 1. Opening (5-8 min)

Real connection. Don't start with an agenda. Start like a normal person.

- Something personal: your location, their location, how their morning was
- Name your preparation: "I looked at your profile/deck/content. This caught my attention..."
- Give them space to respond. Don't rush into business

When you name your preparation, they immediately know this isn't a template call. That trust is earned in the first two minutes.

## 2. Diagnosis (10-15 min)

Listening is a sales technique. Asking questions is the method. Ratio: they talk 75%, you 25%. You steer.

Questions to pick from:
- "How many leads are you getting per week?"
- "Where do they usually drop off?"
- "Do you have a DM strategy, or are you waiting for inbound?"
- "What does your sales cycle look like, from first contact to signature?"
- "What would an ideal outcome look like after six months?"
- "How many hours a week do you have for LinkedIn?"
- "What are your best clients worth annually?"
- "Where is it breaking down: leads, conversion, or follow-up?"
- "How many clients do you want to add in that period?"
- "Do you feel like you're offering too much?"

You're looking for where in the funnel it breaks. Most people think it's a content problem. Almost always it's conversion, in the DMs or on the call itself. Once found, aim your pitch exactly there.

Don't pitch before the diagnosis is complete.

## 3. Summary + entry point (3-5 min)

Summarize the diagnosis in your own words before you pitch.

1. Name the bottleneck: "What I'm hearing is that there are enough leads, but conversion is too low. That's where we need to look first."
2. Name the three pillars (ICP, content, sales) and be direct about which has priority for them
3. Hint at a similar client: "I recognise this from someone else I work with. Can I explain that briefly?"

Similar-client stories without naming names:
> "I work with someone, also a consultant, also a busy schedule, who had exactly this. Her offer was good but she wasn't converting. We simplified it and made it easier to sell. Now she's doing 30k+ a month and has renewed."

**Critical rule:** don't make things up. Don't change numbers. If unsure whether a result is accurate: don't mention it. An exaggerated claim costs more trust than a good story earns.

## 4. The tour (8-12 min)

Share your screen. Show the real working environment, not a slide deck. People don't buy an abstract promise. They buy what they can see.

Intro: "Should I give you a quick tour of how I normally work? Then you'll have a better picture."

| What | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Notion board (kanban + resources) | Always | "This is where we actually work. Posts, sales questions, tasks, everything moves from left to right." |
| Figma workspace | Sometimes | Only if they create visual content or work in Figma |
| Sales deck | Never live | Send after the call. Show the live workspace, not a presentation about it |

Explain: the columns, the pre-built resources (DM scripts, content frameworks), the async structure (Monday-Thursday via Notion, nothing buried in DMs), the biweekly one-hour calls.

## 5. The math (4-5 min)

Make the investment feel small by showing the upside, using their own numbers.

- Ask: "What are your best clients worth? How many would you want to add in six months?"
- Work it out live, out loud, simple:

```
Current situation (e.g. 4 clients x EUR 35k)   EUR 140,000/yr
Goal: +4 clients in 6 months                   +EUR 140,000
Investment (EUR 3k x 6 months)                 EUR 18,000
Net new revenue                                ~EUR 122,000
```

Always calculate conservatively. If you say "four months is realistic," you leave room for a pleasant surprise. Promise results in two months and you create a problem for yourself.

## 6. Naming the investment (2-3 min)

**Golden rule: never name the price halfway through. Always at the end, after the tour and the math.** Before that it feels expensive. After it feels logical.

One sentence, then the structure, then space:

> "The investment is EUR 3,000 per month. We do that for three or six months. If you want to pay upfront, there's a discount on that. I'll send that over afterwards."

You don't defend the price. You don't apologise. You don't explain why it's "actually quite reasonable." Say it. Stop. Wait.

## 7. Reaction check (3-5 min)

Before next steps, ask for their first impression, not a yes/no:

> "What's your first impression? Where is your head going?"

- Ask. Then mouth shut. Let the silence work
- Listen for the real objection, not the surface one
- Reflect back: "What I'm hearing you say is... is that right?"

Skip this and you'll hear the objection in an email two days later, when it's much harder to resolve.

## 9. Close + next steps (3-5 min)

1. Summarise the call in two sentences: what you heard, what you think it solves
2. Send the deck: "In there you'll also see what others share as updates. I find that more useful than written-out case studies."
3. Two business days: "I'll give you two working days. You don't need more time than that for this." More time creates room for doubt that isn't grounded in anything real
4. Make the scarcity real: "Tomorrow an email goes out. I have three spots open for [month]. If it starts getting tight, I'll let you know." Only say this when it's true

If it really isn't a match: refer them to someone else. "If you still feel it isn't quite right, I might know someone who's a better fit." This isn't weak. This builds trust for the long term.

## After the call

- Send the deck (not a new proposal; a proposal is a step backwards and can be confusing)
- Always send a recap with the deck. The customer feels understood and you stay top of mind
- End with a clear next step: follow-up within 5 days, agreement, or a 2-3 month catch-up
- Use PandaDoc or similar for the agreement
