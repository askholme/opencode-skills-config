# DM Sales Principles (Nick Broekema)

## What DM sales is

Phoneless closing: qualifying and sometimes closing deals entirely in LinkedIn DMs.

- Pro: you have time to think about what to say.
- Con: you don't get to know one another well.

You don't have to close in the DMs. Use the same questions as your sales meeting outline. You'll learn much more from sales calls than DMs. The DM conversation's default job is to qualify and book the call.

## The 3-box goal

Every DM sales conversation must tick these boxes, in order:

1. **Assess their current situation** — pains, emotions, problems
2. **Check it's in your wheelhouse** — can you genuinely help with what surfaced?
3. **Summarize, present, close** — reflect back what they told you, present your offer, close

Never pitch before box 1 and 2 are done. Never name price before the summary.

## Behavior rules

- If you answer questions, always end with a question
- Keep it conversational, you're not a robot
- Ask, don't tell (have them do the talking)
- Build a diagnosis, not a strategy
- Be friendly but don't befriend
- Be authoritative, not their buddy
- Keep messages short. One topic per message
- Short affirmations between questions: "Got it, thanks." / "Great."
- Don't give free advice
- Don't oversell. After sending the deck or offer: "Let me know how you'd like to move forward." Then stop talking

## The qualification sequence

The questions below map the lead. You don't need all of them, and not in this exact order. You need enough to tick box 1 and 2.

**Situation:**
- What do you do and for whom?
- What does your offer look like (duration, ICP, average LTV)?
- How many leads per week are you getting right now?
- Where do leads come from today (referrals, inbound, outbound)?

**Problem and history:**
- What's the biggest obstacle to your goal?
- When did you realize this was a problem?
- What have you already tried? What happened?

**Goal and dream outcome:**
- What are your business goals for the next 6 months?
- If we'd work together, what would be your dream outcome?
- What is a Home Run?

The dream outcome question tells you 100% of what you need to know for your summary.

**Urgency (increases value):**
- On a scale from 1 to 10, how urgent is it to work on this NOW instead of 6 months from now?
- What happens if you don't act?

**Budget:**
- What's the price point of your offer? What do clients get for that? (qualifies their economics)
- Are you willing and able to invest in premium-level support to achieve these goals?

It's OK to show you're an expensive/premium option.

## When a lead asks for the price right away

Common situation: a lead asks pricing early in the DM conversation. Observed pattern: leads who get a bare price drop off too fast, they judge on ratio without seeing the upside. Never send the price as a naked number.

First choice: defer to a call.
> "I would need a bit more information to answer that question. Shall we schedule a 20-min call?"

If the lead clearly won't call, or the price question keeps hanging: close in the DM with a structured message that anchors value BEFORE the number. Structure:

1. **Starting point:** "You have [X] followers on LinkedIn, that's a good starting point. You already sell [offer], which says something about the sellability of your offer."
2. **Conservative math with their LTV:** "If we do 'only' 1 sale per month, that's [X]k in new revenue after a year."
3. **How you work, briefly:** 2-3 bullets
4. **Proof:** "One of my clients [result]. Another client [result]." (real results only)
5. **Terms:** "We work together for at least [X months]. After that it's monthly." 
6. **Price, one sentence:** "The investment is [X] per month."
7. **Dual close:** "If this sounds good and you want to start, I can send you everything including an agreement. If you'd rather do a 30-min call next week, we can set that up."

The math makes the investment a no-brainer relative to their own numbers. Example: an offer with 10k LTV sold once a month = 120k in a year. Say this out loud in the message.

## Qualifying for ROI before you pitch

Two questions that must be answered before any pitch or price, because they tell you whether you can deliver ROI (and you're allowed to say that's why you're asking):

- "How do you get leads now? And what's roughly your close rate?"
- "How would you describe the ideal result after 6 months of working together?"

When the answers land, bridge to the call:
> "Ok, this all sounds good! How's your [day] next week? Would be great to spend 30 minutes discussing how I can help."

## Reopening a conversation after a "no"

A lead who said no (often after getting the price too early) is not dead. Reopen weeks or months later by going back to pain and goals, never to the offer:

> "Hey [name], of course! I'm curious: what did you originally want to solve or achieve through content on LinkedIn?"

This restarts the conversation at box 1 (situation and pain) instead of where it died (price). From there, run the normal qualification sequence.

## Mindset: sales is data collection

Many clients under-perform in outreach because they fear being "salesy". Reframe: every DM, follow-up, and rejection is data collection. The worst outcome is not a "no", it's no data. A "no" with a reason improves the offer and the pitch. Repetition builds the confidence, not courage.

When starting out or stuck: dedicate 70% of working time to sales activity (DMs, follow-ups, commenting). It's the fastest path to the first client, the first case study, and real ICP data.

## Pre-call qualification survey

When an inbound message is ambiguous ("would love to learn more") and jumping into sales questions feels awkward: send a short qualification survey BEFORE booking the call.

The survey does three things:
1. Gathers data so you show up prepared
2. Filters out non-serious leads
3. Frames the call as a business discussion, not a casual chat

Always include a budget question, phrased as a fact plus a check:
> "Projects like this start at [X]. Is that within your range?"

Treat the survey as data collection, not gatekeeping.

## Value-first outbound

When a prospect's pain isn't visible on their profile, give value before asking anything. A short personalized Loom video (e.g. a mini audit of their email sequence, profile, or funnel) is memorable, builds trust, and earns the follow-up conversation. Use sparingly, for high-potential leads.

## Confirming inbound leads

When someone inquires ("Details", "tell me more"), confirm your offer first to filter brain-pickers:

> "Great to hear you want to know more about my [program]. Do you mind if I ask you some questions to see if it would be a right fit for you?"

## Inviting, not selling

When qualified, tell them they're qualified instead of selling to them:

> "Excellent. I think you'd be great for my [program]. Would you be interested in receiving my deck with more details?"

Ask permission to share details. This flips the dynamic: they opt in.

Note: this framing doesn't work in every situation (e.g. when talking to a CEO). Match the lead's seniority.

## Closing and next steps

When they say yes, immediately share numbered next steps: calls schedule, invoice, contract, kick-off questions, phone number for support channel. This shows you have a process. Show your excitement ("Stoked to work with you 💪"). Done.

If they choose a package and ask questions first: answer briefly, don't give free advice, don't oversell, keep it short.

## Transparency builds trust

Share real constraints proactively: vacation windows, capacity limits, "I pause collaborations in August." Mentioning this before they commit reads as integrity and creates natural timeline pressure.
