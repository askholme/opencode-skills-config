# Engagement Scripts

Four scripts for turning LinkedIn activity into conversations. All follow the same logic: spot the ICP, connect, qualify with questions, propose a 20-minute call.

## ICP check (do this before every script)

Go through the source (likes, comments, profile views, connection requests) and check:

- Do they have the right role?
- Are they in the right industry?
- Does their company have the right size (FTEs)?
- Check any internal ICP documentation

**Track your leads** (Excel, a CRM like Folk, or just Word) so you don't forget to follow up.

---

## Script 1: Engaging post likes and comments

Send a connection request, then:

**You (V1):**
> Hey [name], thanks for your comment/like on my post about [topic]. Why did it resonate with you?

**You (V2):**
> Hey [name], thanks for interacting with my post about [topic]. What topic about [your solution] would you like to read more of in my content?

Then the qualification ladder, one question per message:

1. "Is this something you / your company is dealing with right now?"
2. "When did you realize this was a problem?"
3. "What have you already tried around [problem]? What happened?"
4. "If you had to describe one goal around [service/pain], what would it be?"

Then the bridge:
> I recently helped [similar ICP] with [problem they mentioned]. They [goal they mentioned]. Would you like to know more about it?

- Yes → "Great, let's do a 20-minute call and I'll tell you more about it." + scheduling link
- No → "No problem. Here's [lead magnet] about [pain]. Hope it helps you. Have a great day!" → follow up after 2 weeks (ask if the lead magnet helped)

## Script 2: Engaging profile views

Send a connection request:

> Hey [name], I noticed [common interest on their profile or content]. Happy to connect with you.

After accepting (also when they accept but don't reply):

> Thanks for connecting. Are you interested in [your content pillar/offer] or were you just looking around?

- **They have a problem** ("We're dealing with [problem], is this something you do?"):
> Yes, I just helped [similar client] getting [desired outcome]. They were struggling with [pain]. Why don't we do a short call so I can tell you more about it?

- **Just looking around:**
> Great. I see you work at [company]. How are you currently doing [question around your offer]?

If a pain surfaces:
> I might be able to help there. Our client [similar ICP] struggled with [similar problem]. We helped them by [solution]. They now [result]. Are you interested in hearing more about this?

Yes → scheduling link.

---

## Script 3: Engaging received connection requests

Accept, then:

> Hey [name], thanks for connecting. Are you interested in my [solution] services or are you just expanding your network?

- **Possibly interested:**
> I recently helped [similar ICP] with [problem they mentioned]. They [desired outcome]. Does this sound like something you would need?

If they ask price:
> I would need a bit more information to answer that question. Shall we schedule a 20-min call?

Never quote price this early. The call answers the price question.

- **Just networking:**
> Glad to hear you enjoy my stuff. What topic would you like to learn more about in my content?

Then: "Is this topic something you or your company struggles with?" → if yes, bridge to the similar-client story and the 20-min call.

## Script 4: Cold outreach (last resort)

Nick doesn't do cold outreach. He focuses on inbound, and that's what he teaches. But if you're just getting started and need clients now, this works. You have to be willing to repeat it.

- Use Sales Navigator to find the right people: filter by role, company size, industry
- Do the research before you write. One specific detail beats a generic opener every time
- Don't expect a high hit rate. That's normal. Volume matters
- The goal is a conversation, not a sale. Keep it short and low-pressure
- Once inbound kicks in, you won't need this anymore

**Step 1 — Context / research:**
> Hey [First Name], I've been looking into [their company / content / recent initiative]. I noticed [specific detail].

**Step 2 — Show understanding of pain / intent:**
> That tells me [pain or opportunity].

**Step 3 — Social proof:**
> I recently helped [similar company / ICP] with [similar challenge]. They went from [pain] to [result], and you can even ask [name/ref] about it if you'd like.

**Step 4 — Light invitation:**
> Would it be worth a short chat to see if this could work for you too?

**Extra cold outreach notes:**
- Structure in short: icebreaker → value proposition → call to action
- No case studies yet? Frame past work experience as a client success story: "I helped a client remove [bottleneck], leading to [result]"
- A/B test your connection requests: blank vs. a short value line. Track which gets the higher acceptance rate
- Volume targets when starting: 10+ cold DMs per day, tracked in a CRM or Sales Navigator

## Warm lead follow-up (variant)

For warm leads (they showed real interest, then went quiet), lower the friction instead of nudging harder:

- Attempt 1: "To make it easier, here's my meeting link."
- Attempt 2: "Is this still relevant for you?"
- Cadence: every 2 business days, 3-4 attempts max. Then move on.

For generic or likely automated inbound connection messages: one gentle nudge after 2 days, nothing more.

## Follow-up cadence (all scripts)

Most deals are closed in the follow-ups. People are busy. Life happens. Don't feel rejected. Follow up and detach from the emotional outcome.

**After 2 days:**
Best option: leave a voice note (nobody ignores those). Text options:
- "Thoughts?"
- "[name]?"

**After 4 days:**
> Anything back on this?

**After 6 days:**
> I know, LinkedIn messaging is not the best. How about we just jump on a 20-min call to talk about solving [client problem]?

+ scheduling link.

No response after that: dead lead. NEXT.
